# Kinetic Energy Insoles

Shoe insoles that generate electricity when you walk, plus a dock that stores the energy. GCSE Design & Technology project (2021–2022).

## Why

Every step you take puts energy into the ground and it's just lost. I wanted to capture some of it.

## How it works

- Each insole has 10 piezoelectric discs wired in series, so their voltages add up.
- The charge goes into a capacitor.
- Shoes connect to a charging dock through magnetic contacts.
- Before the final build, a breadboard test charged the capacitor to 2 V and lit an LED, which proved the idea worked.

## Design decisions

- **Where the force goes:** pressing a disc off-centre gave much less voltage, so I designed flexible SolidWorks parts that push on the centre of each disc.
- **Material:** an insole takes 500–1000 N every step. I load-tested aluminium, acrylic and Delrin. Delrin won because it springs back, doesn't snap, and laser cuts accurately.
- **Size:** 150 × 60 mm, based on foot measurement data, so it fits most adult shoes under a normal comfort insole.

It took a lot of design → laser cut → 3D print cycles to get the parts right.
